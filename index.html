Getting research out of the paper

Reading something promising and finding out what it takes to run it on real data — several years of that, much of it in enterprise R&D. The gap between a published result and a working prototype is where I've learned the most.

  • NL2SQL, so non-technical teams could query a warehouse in plain language.
  • Agentic workflows for multi-step internal processes, and GNNs over relational data.
  • A path I keep reusing: paper → notebook → API → something a team can try.

Where I started

Optimization & forecasting

I came up through operations research at inLab FIB — UPC, working on how a city moves. I still reach for these tools more often than people expect — plenty of problems labelled "AI" are constraint problems in disguise.

  • Forecasting bus passenger occupancy from millions of ticket validations.
  • Hard-constrained vehicle routing to reduce the fleet needed, with CPLEX and metaheuristics.
  • Shared-taxi routing for people with reduced mobility, tested on a Barcelona case study.
